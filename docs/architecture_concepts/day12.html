Microservices

Microservices solve an organizational problem, not a technical one. Understanding this distinction prevents millions of dollars in wrong architecture decisions.

1
The Counter-Narrative
Amazon Prime Video · 2023

Amazon Prime Video moved their video quality monitoring service from microservices back to a monolith. Costs dropped 90%. Not surprising — the same pattern has appeared at multiple companies. Sometimes the "best practice" architecture isn't the right architecture.

2
The Real Problem Microservices Solve
Core Insight

Microservices solve an organizational problem, not a technical one. The bottleneck microservices address is team coordination and deployment independence — not raw system performance.

With 40 engineers and 6 teams all committing to the same codebase: blocking, broken tests, deployment coordination hell. Microservices give each team their own service, repo, deploy pipeline, release schedule.

3
The Fundamental Rule
Conway's Law in Practice

One team, one service.

Two teams own one service = wrong boundary drawn. One team owns four services = four modules pretending to be services. Your service boundaries should mirror your team boundaries — this is Conway's Law.

4
Real Costs Nobody Mentions
⚠ Hidden Tax

I've seen teams where 60% of engineering time went to infrastructure instead of features. Microservices are a multiplier — on both productivity and operational overhead.

① Network ≠ Function Calls

Function calls: nanoseconds, can't fail, no timeout needed.
Network calls: 1–10ms, can fail, can timeout, need retry logic, circuit breakers, fallbacks.
Every service boundary you add multiplies this cost.

② Distributed Transactions

In a monolith: BEGIN; do stuff; COMMIT;
In microservices: saga pattern with compensating actions across services. If step 3 of 5 fails, you need to undo steps 1 and 2 manually. Extremely hard to get right.

③ Debugging is Detective Work

Monolith: grep logs.
Microservices: distributed tracing (Jaeger, Zipkin) + correlation IDs tracked across 7 different log streams in 7 different systems.

④ Operational Overhead Multiplies

15 services = 15 deploy pipelines, 15 monitoring dashboards, 15 alert sets, possibly 15 databases. Each service needs SLOs, runbooks, on-call rotation coverage.

5
When Microservices Actually Make Sense
✅ Use Microservices When
  • Multiple teams need independent deploys — this is signal #1
  • Components have genuinely different scaling needs
  • Different tech stacks are genuinely required per component
  • Fault isolation is critical (payments must survive notification outage)
❌ Don't Use Microservices When
  • Single team — Conway's Law says don't
  • Early stage — product boundaries still changing
  • Bottleneck is product velocity, not engineering scale
6
Practical Path
1

Well-Structured Monolith

Clean module boundaries, interfaces, logical DB separation. This is what Shopify does at billions in GMV. The foundation must be right before splitting.

2

Extract First Service (Real Pain)

Good first extractions: auth/identity, email/notifications, file upload. These are well-understood, stateless, and high-traffic. Feel the operational tax before committing.

3

Extract More as Team Grows

Let Conway's Law guide you. As you hire more teams, extract services that match new team boundaries. Don't extract in anticipation — extract in response to real pain.

7
Prerequisites Before First Extraction

These aren't optional. Without them, your first microservice extraction will be painful and fragile. Build them first in the monolith.

⚠ Don't Skip These

If you extract a service without these prerequisites, debugging will be a nightmare. You'll spend more time on infrastructure than the feature that motivated the extraction in the first place.
